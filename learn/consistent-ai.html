Interactive lesson

Consistent AI: one standard, not twenty habits.

When everyone uses AI their own way, quality depends on who did the work. Learn how shared templates, a register of AI uses and clear owners make results consistent and safe.

  • About 20 minutes
  • Beginner friendly
  • No account required
  • Original diagrams

Why consistency matters

Give three people the same task and the same AI tool, and you'll get three different results: different lengths, different facts emphasised, sometimes invented figures. That's a problem when the output reaches trustees, funders or the people you support.

Shared templates

Tested prompts for common tasks, with the rules built in.

A register of AI uses

One list of where AI is used, by whom, with which tool and data.

Clear owners

Each template and use has a named role who keeps it working.

Source feedback (fictional)

All 50 attendees completed the feedback form. Of these, 42 rated the evening good or excellent. The speakers received the most positive comments. Six attendees reported problems with step-free access. Some comments asked for better food options, different timings and another event next year. The events team agreed to confirm an accessible venue before booking the next event.

These outputs were written for the lesson. The switch compares worked examples; it is not a test proving that a template prevents errors. Check each summary against the source. No AI model is called.

Key ideas to take away

          
    
            
  • Template the common tasks. A tested prompt for each recurring task beats everyone writing their own.
    • 
            
  • Build the rules into the template. “Only use the information provided” and “say when something isn't there” travel with every use.
    • 
            
  • Keep a register of AI uses. You can't manage, check or explain what you can't see.
    • 
            
  • Give everything an owner. Templates and tools need someone to update them when the model, the task or the rules change.
    • 
            
  • Make the right way the easy way. Put templates where people already work, and keep the library short.
    •

Version your templates

              
Treat a template like any shared document: number versions, note what changed, and re-test it against a few saved examples when the tool or model is updated (see Lesson 07).

              
Training beats policing

              
Short, practical sessions on the templates and the reasons behind the rules do more for consistency than monitoring people.
