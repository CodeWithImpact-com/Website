AI in your processes: improve the work you already do.

AI adds most value inside processes you already run, not as a separate experiment. Learn to map a process, choose where AI helps, and keep people at the points that matter.

  • About 20 minutes
  • Beginner friendly
  • No account required
  • Original diagrams

Map first, then decide step by step

Write the process down as it really happens today, step by step. Then, for each step, ask two questions: how much judgement does it need, and what happens if it goes wrong?

Keep human

Judgement about people, safeguarding, or anything hard to undo.

AI drafts, person checks

Reading, summarising, sorting and drafting, where a quick check catches errors.

Automate

Routine, low-risk, identical every time, and easy to spot and fix if wrong.

Measure before and after. Time how long the process takes now and count its errors. Without a baseline you can't tell whether AI helped.

Key ideas to take away

  • Map the real process first. Write down what actually happens today, including the workarounds.
  • Match the role to the risk. Automate the routine, assist the judgement-heavy, keep decisions about people human.
  • Design the checkpoint, not just the AI step. Say who checks, what they check, and what they do when something's wrong.
  • Measure a baseline. Time and errors before AI, then the same measures after.
  • Tell people what changed. Staff, volunteers and the people you serve should know where AI is used.

A simple risk test for each step

QuestionIf yes
Does it decide something about a person?Keep a person in charge of the decision.
Would an error be hard to spot or undo?Add a check before the output is used.
Does it use personal or sensitive data?Approved tools only; consider an impact assessment.
Is it identical every time and low impact?A good candidate for automation.

Watch for automation bias

People tend to accept a machine's suggestion, especially when busy. Make checks meaningful: give reviewers time, show them the source, and occasionally include a known error to confirm they're still catching them.

Workflows before agents

A fixed sequence of steps you design is easier to test than an agent that chooses its own route. Lesson 06 covers when an agent is worth it.
