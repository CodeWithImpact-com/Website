Why your support matters

Charities are using AI. Almost none use it for analysis.

Research charities hold data that could change lives, but most don't have the people or budget to use it. That is the gap we work in.

76%

of UK charities used AI tools in 2025, up from 61% the year before.1

13%

use it to analyse numerical data, and only 4% for predictive analysis.2

64%

report poor or no AI skills in their organisation.3

What Code With Impact gives

Real tools, built and handed over, free of charge.

For research charities

Data and AI tools that stay useful

Dashboards, data cleaning, AI-assisted search and automation, built with the partner and documented so their team can keep using them. Our current work includes a dashboard with MQ Mental Health Research.

For clinicians and researchers

Help with messy data

Preparing and cleaning research data so analysis can start, for example supporting a clinical meta-analysis.

For everyone

Free Learn AI and Learn ML paths

Interactive learning on our website, open to anyone, with every source properly credited.

For volunteers and students

A supported way to use your skills for good

Professionals and students work together on real health challenges, with a first task, a reviewer and a mentor.

Our volunteers come largely from quantitative finance and risk, where models are tested, validated and documented before anyone relies on them. We bring that discipline to every tool we build.

Where we sit

A focused gap: ongoing builds for health research.

The UK has excellent organisations connecting skilled volunteers with charities, such as DataKind UK, Pro Bono Economics and the Royal Statistical Society's Statisticians for Society. Most serve the whole voluntary sector, or offer advice and one-off projects. We focus on one thing: building and maintaining data and AI tools with health and medical research organisations.

Why social initiatives fail

Good programmes close. We're designing so we don't.

In 2026, Reach Volunteering, the UK's largest skills-based volunteering charity, announced it would close despite record demand, because funders had shifted away from the services that connect people and charities.4 A group of funders stepped in to keep it open.5 Warwick's Data Science for Social Good programme, which brought data science teams to charities and public bodies, is no longer running.6 The pattern is common: the work is valued, but the costs behind it go unfunded.

What we do about it

Running costs nobody funds

Hosting, software and admin are invisible, so grants rarely cover them.

Many small supporters

Coffees, our upcoming shop, training and grants, so no single funder decides our future.

Volunteers burn out or move on

Work depends on a few people with limited time.

Small tasks, shared methods

Teams form around defined projects, with onboarding and a reviewer, so new people can pick work up.

Tools without an owner

A tool built by volunteers can stop working when they leave.

Handover built in

Every build ends with documentation, training and an agreed owner on the partner's side.

Everything rests on the founder

One person's time and money hold the whole thing up.

Shared leadership

Named owners for delivery, quality, operations and funding, with a clear path to becoming a registered organisation.
